# ORI-OS Market, Pricing & GDPR Strategy

## 1. Purpose and High-Level Goals

ORI-OS is an AI-powered GTM operating system that unifies lead intelligence, CRM, outbound campaigns, automation, analytics, SEO and content for B2B teams (agencies, SaaS, services). The goals of this document are:

- To define a market and pricing strategy that maximises revenue while remaining realistic for a small SaaS and aligned with GDPR and other data-protection norms.
- To describe the target markets, ICPs and messaging for ORI-OS in EU and non-EU regions (especially US and LATAM).
- To re-structure the legal base of the app (privacy, terms, DPA, security) so it can support compliant operations.
- To provide a technical/legal checklist for the product.
- To outline a multi-agent Codex plan that can implement and iterate on all of the above in a structured, token-efficient way.


## 2. Target Markets and ICPs

### 2.1 EU Context (Baseline)

- ORI-OS is currently operated from Poland (EU). Any processing of personal data is subject to GDPR principles, even when targeting non-EU markets.
- Compliance cannot be ignored; however, a “minimum viable compliance” approach is possible before deeper audits.

### 2.2 Primary ICP for EU: International Recruiting Agencies

- Agencies placing talent across borders for B2B clients.
- Pain points: fragmented tools (spreadsheets, generic CRM, ATS, email tools), manual reporting, pressure to comply with GDPR when handling candidates and client contacts.
- ORI-OS value: unified CRM + outreach + automation + analytics + SEO studio; clear pipelines per client and role; automation for repetitive tasks; reporting to prove value.

### 2.3 Non-EU Focus: US and LATAM B2B

Because GDPR applies to the company in Poland, non-EU targeting is a *commercial* decision, not a way to evade basic GDPR principles. However, enforcement pressure and buyer expectations differ.

#### 2.3.1 US Market

- No omnibus federal privacy law equivalent to GDPR; there is a patchwork of sectoral federal laws and state-level consumer privacy acts (CCPA/CPRA, Colorado, Virginia, etc.).
- For a small B2B SaaS dealing with business contacts (not consumer-scale data), many state laws only apply after certain thresholds (revenue, number of consumers, volume of data).
- This makes US SMBs and agencies relatively accessible, provided good practices and honest privacy notices are in place.

#### 2.3.2 LATAM Market

- Many countries (Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, etc.) have modern data-protection laws, often inspired by GDPR, and several are tightening AI and cross-border transfer rules.
- Enforcement and vendor-risk processes in SMBs are generally less formal than in EU mid/large enterprises, making them good early-stage targets.


## 3. ICPs for ORI-OS (EU + US/LATAM)

### 3.1 Core EU ICP

1. **International recruiting agencies** (3–25 people)
   - Use cases: prospecting new clients, managing vacancies, outreach to candidates, reporting.
   - Key modules: Intelligence, CRM (Contacts/Companies/Deals), Engagement, Automation, Analytics, SEO Studio.

### 3.2 US/LATAM ICPs (10 Profiles)

1. **SaaS appointment-setting & outbound agencies (US)**
   - Sell demos/discovery calls to SaaS; need structured campaigns and per-client ROI reporting.
2. **LATAM outbound/growth agencies (Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, Chile, etc.)**
   - Generate pipeline for services/SaaS; need multi-client pipelines and affordable tooling.
3. **Digital marketing / growth boutiques**
   - Manage multi-channel campaigns and leads for clients; need reporting and automation.
4. **Non-EU tech recruiting boutiques**
   - Place tech talent in local/regional markets; similar needs to EU recruiting but without EU candidate volume.
5. **Early-stage B2B SaaS teams (founder-led sales)**
   - Need prospecting, experiments in messaging, pipeline and MRR analytics.
6. **Boutique consultancies (strategy, HR, operations)**
   - Sell project-based work; require clear opportunity pipelines and outreach.
7. **Commercial real-estate / office-space lead-gen agencies (B2B)**
   - Generate leads for office/logistics spaces; need deal tracking and campaigns.
8. **Outsourced SDR providers**
   - Run outbound for several clients; need per-client structure and reporting.
9. **Productized service providers (cold email, SEO, RevOps-as-a-service)**
   - Deliver repeatable service packages using templates, workflows, dashboards.
10. **SEO/content inbound agencies**
    - Need SEO Studio + Content Library integrated with CRM and Analytics to show attribution.

## 5. Pricing Strategy

Pricing must be competitive against agency CRMs and outbound platforms (often ~12–65 USD per user/month, or flat 47–97 USD/month for agency plans), while leaving margin to invest in legal/compliance work.

### 5.1 Suggested Plans

1. **Solo** – freelancers and founders
   - Price: $49/month (flat).
   - 1 user, 1 workspace.
   - Included: core modules (Intelligence limited, CRM, Engagement, basic Automation, Analytics, SEO Studio for 1 domain).

2. **Team** – small agencies / SaaS GTM teams
   - Price: $149/month (up to 5 users).
   - More contacts, workflows, campaigns, 3 SEO domains, reporting by client/tag.

3. **Agency** – multi-client agencies / SDR providers
   - Price: $299/month (up to ~15 users, extensible).
   - Multi-client views, higher limits, multi-workspace or per-client segmentation, priority support.

4. **Compliance Pack (add-on, for demanding clients)**
   - One-off fee: $750–$1,500.
   - Includes: custom DPA review, vendor questionnaires support, extended security documentation.

This tiering is:

- Accessible to freelancers and small teams.
- Competitive vs typical agency CRMs and “agency OS” tools.
- Designed to fund initial GDPR/legal reviews without overpricing.


## 6. Legal and GDPR Restructuring

### 6.1 Roles (Controller vs Processor)

- ORI-OS (company in Poland) is:
  - **Controller** for data of its own users and website visitors.
  - **Processor** for data that customers upload (client contacts, candidates, CRM records).
- Freelance scenarios:
  - Freelancer using ORI-OS internally is often a **controller** for his/her own data and may be a processor or joint controller for client-provided data.

### 6.2 Privacy Policy Structure (Summary)

Key elements:

- Identity and contact of controller; explanation of controller/processor roles.
- Categories of data: account data, CRM/recruiting data, usage/log data, communications, SEO/integration data.
- Purposes and legal bases (contract, legitimate interest, consent where needed).
- Data sharing and sub-processors; international transfers and safeguards.
- Retention policies and deletion/export tools.
- Data subject rights (access, rectification, deletion, portability, restriction, objection, complaints).
- Security overview.
- Automated decision-making (if using AI scoring) explanation.

### 6.3 Terms of Service Adjustments

- Clarify intended use: B2B agencies, SaaS, services.
- Acceptable Use: no spam, require lawful basis for campaigns, respect opt-outs.
- Data Protection section: reference to DPA, roles, responsibilities of customers.
- Termination: export and deletion/anonymisation of customer data within defined timeframe.

### 6.4 Data Processing Agreement (DPA) Essentials

- Define subject matter, roles (controller/processor), duration.
- Nature and purpose of processing (CRM, outreach, analytics, SEO, automation).
- Categories of data and data subjects.
- Customer responsibilities (lawful basis, notices, configuration).
- ORI-OS obligations: process on instructions, confidentiality, security, sub-processors, assistance, breach notification, international transfers.
- Return/deletion of data and limited backups.
- Audit and information rights aligned with GDPR Article 28.

### 6.5 Security Page

- Honest description of infrastructure, encryption, access control, backups, monitoring, incident response.
- Do **not** claim SOC 2/ISO/GDPR “certified” status until actually obtained.


## 7. Technical & Product Checklist (Minimum Compliance)

### 7.1 Data Model and Features

- Implement **export** functionality for Contacts, Companies, Deals, Campaigns/Sequences (CSV/JSON from UI and/or API).
- Implement **delete/anonymise** functionality for Contacts/Candidates, including related emails and logs when feasible.
- Maintain minimal audit logs (e.g. “deleted at X by user Y”) for security and accountability.
- Include fields for **last interaction** and **status** per contact/candidate; consider retention alerts or automation (e.g. flag records older than X months).
- Implement RBAC with Owner/Admin/Member roles and clear permissions on viewing/exporting/deleting data, especially for multi-client agencies.

### 7.2 Internal Documentation

- Create a **data map**: entities, fields, flows, sub-processors, retention.
- Maintain a sub-processor list and update policy.
- Document incident response steps and data subject request procedures.

### 7.3 Security Controls

- TLS everywhere; hashed passwords; 2FA when possible.
- Regular encrypted backups with tested restore procedures.
- Restricted production access based on least privilege; logging of access and key actions.


## 8. Multi-Agent Codex Plan

The goal is to have Codex (or a similar multi-agent system) work in parallel on different aspects of ORI-OS while keeping a single source of truth (this .md) and minimising hallucinations and token waste.

### 8.1 Core Principles

- **Single source of truth:** this markdown file is the canonical context about market, pricing, legal and product direction.
- **Specialised agents:** each agent focuses on a narrow domain (product, legal, GTM, etc.) and works with only the relevant sections.
- **Project Manager (PM) agent:** supervises tasks, checks outputs against this file, and coordinates corrections.
- **Short, well-scoped tasks:** each agent works in small iterations, returning artefacts (code, docs) that others can build upon.

### 8.2 Agents and Responsibilities

1. **PM / Orchestrator Agent**
   - Reads this .md fully and maintains a global task list.
   - Splits work into phases and sub-tasks, assigns them to specialised agents.
   - Validates that outputs align with:
     - target ICPs and pricing,
     - GDPR/legal constraints,
     - technical checklist.
   - Rejects or flags outputs with hallucinations, inconsistencies or scope creep.

2. **Product Architecture Agent**
   - Defines and maintains:
     - entity model (Users, Workspaces, Contacts, Companies, Deals, Campaigns, Workflows, SEO Projects, Content Items),
     - relationships (multi-client handling, tenant isolation),
     - API surface (Next.js routes, Prisma schema, etc.).
   - Coordinates with Legal Agent to ensure fields/flows support export, deletion and audit requirements.

3. **Legal & Compliance Agent**
   - Uses sections 6 and 7 of this file as constraints.
   - Drafts/maintains Privacy Policy, Terms, DPA and Security page.
   - Defines concrete requirements for features (e.g. data export endpoints, deletion flows, log retention) that Product Architecture and Implementation Agents must satisfy.

4. **Market & Pricing / GTM Agent**
   - Uses sections 2–5.
   - Refines ICP definitions, messages, landing copy, and pricing tiers.
   - Produces content for website: home page hero, ICP-specific blocks, pricing page copy, FAQs.
   - Works with UX Agent to turn messaging into layouts.

5. **UX & Copy Agent**
   - Designs page structure (wireframes) for:
     - Landing page (hero + ICP sections + CTA),
     - Pricing page,
     - Legal pages,
     - Dashboard onboarding flows.
   - Translates GTM Agent’s messaging into UI text, tooltips, and empty-state copy.

6. **Backend Implementation Agent**
   - Implements schemas and APIs according to Product Architecture Agent:
     - Data export, delete/anonymise, retention flags,
     - RBAC and auth,
     - logging and audit.
   - Coordinates with Security Agent to align with best practices.

7. **Frontend Implementation Agent**
   - Builds React/Next.js UI for dashboard modules, landing, pricing and settings.
   - Implements UX flows defined by UX Agent.
   - Integrates export/delete/retention and multi-client views.

8. **Security & DevOps Agent**
   - Designs infrastructure (cloud provider, regions, networking).
   - Implements TLS, backups, CI/CD, monitoring and incident response hooks.
   - Produces a concise Security page based on actual controls.

9. **Testing / QA Agent**
   - Writes test plans and automated tests:
     - unit/integration tests for export/delete/retention,
     - regression tests for pricing/plan logic,
     - snapshot tests for legal pages.
   - Validates features against Legal & Technical checklist.

10. **Analytics & Data Agent**
   - Designs event schema and analytics tables (for revenue, leads, campaigns and SEO performance).
   - Ensures analytics respect data minimisation and retention requirements.

### 8.3 Phased Workflow

**Phase 0 – Context ingestion**
- PM Agent ingests this .md and creates a high-level roadmap.

**Phase 1 – Foundations**
- Product Architecture Agent defines data model and core APIs.
- Legal Agent refines Privacy/Terms/DPA baselines and lists mandatory product features.

**Phase 2 – GTM & UX**
- Market & Pricing Agent finalises ICPs and pricing tiers.
- UX & Copy Agent designs landing/pricing/legal pages and dashboard skeleton.

**Phase 3 – Implementation**
- Backend and Frontend Agents implement features iteratively.
- Security & DevOps Agent sets infrastructure, logging and backups.

**Phase 4 – Testing & Compliance Check**
- QA Agent validates against technical checklist and legal constraints.
- Legal Agent re-checks that implemented flows (export/delete/retention) match the DPA and Privacy Policy.

**Phase 5 – Iteration and Scaling**
- PM Agent coordinates feedback from early users.
- Market/Pricing Agent adjusts plans as real usage and willingness to pay are observed.
- Legal Agent schedules periodic micro-reviews and updates documents.

### 8.4 Token-Efficiency Strategies

- Each agent works with **only the relevant sections** of this .md plus minimal local context.
- PM Agent keeps a short “current task context” document per agent instead of re-sending the whole file.
- Artefacts (schemas, copy, documents) are treated as reusable context chunks; Codex agents reference them instead of regenerating.
